# Business use case — OEE Chat Assistant

**Customer:** Veridion Chemicals, specialty polymers manufacturer, Calder Valley Plant.
**Owner:** Business / Solutions.

## The pitch

Production supervisors at Calder Valley currently pull OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness)
numbers from a separate reporting tool, hours after a shift ends, with no easy way to see
*why* OEE dropped — was it downtime, slow running, or scrap? Veridion wants a chat interface
where anyone on the floor can ask about a line's OEE in plain language, get an answer grounded
in CDF data, and — when the answer points to a specific cause — fix it immediately from the
same conversation.

## Customer-facing promise

> **See real-time OEE for any line, and automatically generate a corrective work order the
> moment OEE drops below target.**

This is the line Sales has used in the Veridion account conversation and in the generic
"OEE Chat Assistant" one-pager shown to prospects.

## Example scenario

A production supervisor at Calder Valley asks:

> "What's OEE for Line 3 this shift, and what's driving the loss?"

The product answers using the plant's actual CDF data — the OEE composite and its
Availability / Performance / Quality components on `asset_line3`, plus the downtime
activities behind any Availability loss (e.g. the unplanned stop on Reactor R-301) — and
cites what it used.

If the answer shows OEE has dropped below the shift target, the customer-facing promise is
that the supervisor can turn that straight into a corrective work order without leaving the
chat.

## Why this matters to the business

- Differentiator against competitors who only offer OEE dashboards: Veridion is buying an
  **assistant that can act on an OEE drop**, not just a slower way to see the same chart.
- "Automatically generate a work order on OEE drop" is the specific capability that came up
  in the Veridion renewal conversation and is expected to be referenced again in the Q4
  business review.
