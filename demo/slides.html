What worked

There is one place to read the epic.

Business, Product, and Tech each have a page. You can ask a question without opening eight files.

Give the wiki that credit in one breath, then go to the failures. Do not linger.
Downside

The overview declares the epic ready.

“Create a work order” becomes “a path to close the loop.” Disagreements become wording. The badge stays green.

This is the false green. The compilation is doing the smoothing, not the sources.
Downside

The default question reads that page.

The PRD and the ADR are in the wiki. The answer does not open them unless you force it to.

Same wiki, two answers. Overview only says yes. Every page says Business, Product, and Tech disagree.
Downside

Lint does not stop anyone.

It can list the tension and still leave kickoff status on Ready. There is no sign-off, and no version a sign-off would point at.

Health: usable. That phrase is the product failing politely.
Downside

The intern’s finding never reaches the overview.

About 40% of demo tenants have no file links. Lint does not mention it. The board slide stays a happy path.

Show that Every page can see the note and Overview only cannot. A wiki that compiles “the story” drops the inconvenient fact.
Downside · scalability

Every ingest rewrites the wiki.

Eight sources became a dozen compiled pages. The next sprint adds tickets and another epic. The health check rereads the compilation, and the compilation gets smoother.

Tie to the DevCon theme without giving the whole later talk. Scale here means the cost of rereading and rewriting, not token throughput.
Not claimed today

This is not a gate on sprint planning.

Putting more agents on this wiki does not fix the green status. The piece that holds is a record of the claims, and a check the writer cannot override.

If asked “what next”: agents are the next experiment and they are not the arrival. Claim records plus a deterministic check on the change are. Do not open that design unless they ask.
